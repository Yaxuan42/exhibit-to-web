亚轩的梯子：一个站名的来历 Yaxuan’s Ladder: On the Name

关于那把梯子，以及为什么选它做站名 On Wittgenstein’s ladder, and why this notebook bears its name.

2026 · 04 · 18 · 约 800 字 ~ 3 min read

给博客取名是一件被人低估的事。名字不只是标签，它也设定自我审视的坐标——你写什么、为什么写、写到什么程度才算数。

这个站点叫"亚轩的梯子"。梯子的说法来自维特根斯坦。他在《逻辑哲学论》接近尾声处写下了这段话——一本以严格的逻辑命题结构写成的书，快结束时却突然让这些命题自我消解：

我的命题应当是以如下方式来起阐明作用的：任何理解我的人，当他用这些命题为梯级而超越了它们时，就会终于认识到它们是无意义的。可以说，在登上高处之后他必须把梯子扔掉。他必须超越这些命题，然后他就会正确看待世界。 维特根斯坦 · 逻辑哲学论 § 6.54

一本书花了七十几页反复搭建一套逻辑，最后告诉读者：它是错的，把它扔掉吧。乍看是自毁，细想是坦诚——任何用于攀登的工具，都会在攀登完成之后失去意义。工具不是目的，它是目的的台阶。

为什么用这个比喻做站名

我是一个产品负责人，也写代码——或者说，在 AI 把写代码这件事变成廉价之后，我仍然愿意动手写代码。每天被我用掉又扔掉的东西非常多：一个产品假设、一段 prompt、一次架构草图、一篇还没发出去的长文。它们大多数都不是终点，只是为了让我看清下一步的立足点。

写博客也是这样。我不相信一篇文章能给出终极答案，也不指望自己两年后仍然同意今天写下的结论。但文章值得写下来——因为它构成了那一阶梯子，没有它我看不到再上一层。

所以"梯子"对我来说有三层意思：

一 · 工具感

博客不是作品集，是思考工具。发出去的文字首先是给我自己看的。如果某一天我不再需要它，那恰好说明它完成了它的任务。

二 · 临时性

允许自己写得不究竟。维特根斯坦不要求他的命题永远正确，他只要求它们能支撑你登上下一阶。这对一个写作者来说是巨大的解脱。

三 · 方向感

梯子是向上的。它不是漫游的地图，它暗示一个方向——哪怕这个方向你描述不清楚。

维基百科这个词条下还有一段旁注——说这个比喻并非维特根斯坦首创，叔本华、塞克斯都·恩披里柯、甚至庄子，都用过类似的意象。庄子说"得鱼而忘筌，得意而忘言"：抓到鱼就可以把网扔了，理解了意思就可以把话扔了。和梯子是同一件事。

这个博客如果做得足够长久，总有一天我会回过头看今天这篇，觉得它既朴素又有点好笑。那会是一个好消息——说明梯子起了作用。

Naming a blog is an underrated act. A name is not just a label; it sets the coordinates of self-examination — what you write, why, and how seriously.

This site is called Yaxuan’s Ladder. The ladder comes from Wittgenstein. Near the end of the Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus — a book written in the form of rigorous logical propositions — he abruptly lets those propositions dissolve themselves:

My propositions serve as elucidations in the following way: anyone who understands me eventually recognizes them as nonsensical, when he has used them — as steps — to climb beyond them. He must, so to speak, throw away the ladder after he has climbed up it. He must transcend these propositions, and then he will see the world aright. Wittgenstein · Tractatus § 6.54

Seventy pages of careful logic, only to be told at the end: it’s wrong, throw it away. At first this reads like self-destruction. On reflection it is simply honest — any tool used for climbing loses its meaning once the climb is done. The tool is not the goal; it is a step toward the goal.

Why this metaphor for the name

I’m a product lead who also codes — or, more precisely, someone who still wants to write code after AI has made writing code cheap. I generate and discard a great deal every day: product hypotheses, prompts, architecture sketches, half-written essays. Most are not endpoints; they are footholds from which to see where to step next.

A blog is the same. I don’t believe a single post can deliver a final answer, nor do I expect to still agree with myself two years from now. But writing things down is worth it — each post becomes one rung of the ladder, and without it I cannot see the next.

So "ladder" carries three meanings for me:

I · Tool

A blog is not a portfolio. It is a thinking tool. The words go out, but they are first of all for me. If one day I no longer need a particular post, that is exactly the sign that it has done its work.

II · Provisionality

Permission to be unfinished. Wittgenstein doesn’t ask that his propositions be eternally true — only that they support the next step. For a writer, that is an enormous relief.

III · Direction

A ladder points up. It is not a map for wandering; it implies a direction, even if the direction can’t be fully articulated.

A footnote on the Wikipedia entry notes that the metaphor was not Wittgenstein’s invention — Schopenhauer, Sextus Empiricus, and even Zhuangzi used the same figure. Zhuangzi wrote: once the fish is caught, the trap is forgotten; once the meaning is grasped, the words are forgotten. Same ladder, different century.

If this blog lasts long enough, one day I will come back to this post and find it both earnest and a little funny. That would be good news — it would mean the ladder did its job.
